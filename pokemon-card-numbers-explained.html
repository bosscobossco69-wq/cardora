What the number on a Pokemon card actually means

"166/165" is genuine. "132/106" is not. Both are higher than the set total, which is exactly why a rule as simple as "the first number can't be bigger" gets honest sellers rejected and scammers believed.

Short answer

The number is position / size of that set. Cards above the set size are called secret rares and are printed that way on purpose. A number above the total proves nothing in either direction. What matters is whether that exact number exists in that set's official card list.

1. The format, read out loud

Every legally printed Pokemon card carries a slash number, usually near the bottom edge beside the copyright line. 012/078 reads as "card 12 of the 78-card set I belong to". The denominator is the size of one set, not of the game. Different sets have different totals, so the same Pokémon can carry completely different numbers across releases.

Because the denominator is set-specific, a card number is only meaningful together with its set name. Reading "132" or "166" in isolation tells you nothing at all; you need the set logo printed on the card first.

2. Why genuine cards go over the total

Since the Neo Era, Pokemon sets have printed chase cards outside the numbered run. They are called secret rares, and the numbering deliberately exceeds the set size, starting at "total + 1".

The difference between the second and third row is not arithmetic. 132/106 fails because no official set contains that entry at all, not because 132 is bigger than 106.

3. The myth that costs sellers money

"If the first number is bigger than the set size, it's fake."

This rule is wrong, and it circulates on marketplaces, in chat groups and in some seller checklists. Applied literally, it rejects every secret rare, ultra rare and gold card in print - including some of the most valuable cards in the hobby. A buyer who believes it talks sellers down; a seller who believes it underprices. Counterfeiters benefit from the confusion, because a genuine 207/165 gets waved away as "impossible" while a well-made fake with a plausible number passes.

4. What actually verifies a number

  1. Read the set logo on the bottom-left of the card, not the artwork. That identifies which card list you have to check.
  2. Open that set's official card list and look for your card's collector number. If the set has secret rares, the list continues past the total - a complete list shows where it stops.
  3. Scan it with the official Pokemon TCG app. The free app's scan feature compares the physical card against the official database and is the fastest single check available to a buyer holding the card. This is what a human reviewer can compare a listing against.
  4. Then check the physical tells. A correct number on a badly printed card is still a fake - see how to spot a fake Pokemon card.

Frequently asked questions

Is a Pokemon card numbered higher than the set size fake?

No. Secret rares are printed above the set total on purpose - Pokemon Card 151 runs to 165 and prints secret rares from 166/165 up to 207/165. The number must exist in that set's official list; exceeding the set size by itself is normal.

Is 132/106 Pikachu ex real?

That specific printing is recorded in the ungraded.com Fake Card Database as a counterfeit. If you are offered one, assume it is fake unless a seller can show the card, the back, and an official app scan recognising it.

What does the number after the slash mean?

The size of the set the card was printed in, not the size of the whole game and not a rarity indicator. Two cards from different sets can share the same first number and mean entirely different things.

Can the official Pokemon TCG app tell me a card is real?

The app recognises the card against the official database, which is the strongest check available without lab equipment - and it can be fooled by good scans of genuine reference images. Treat it as one layer: scan result, print quality, card stock and card back all have to agree.

Why do English and Japanese versions of "the same" card have different numbers?

They are different sets with different totals and different secret rare runs. Always pair the number with the set logo on the physical card, never with the character name alone.
