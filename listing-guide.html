How to photograph a card so buyers trust the listing

A buyer cannot hold the card before paying, so the photos do the work a shop counter cannot. Six to eight honest shots settle most questions before they are asked, and cut the back-and-forth that kills a sale.

Two things this page is not

It is not a camera. Trixovel hosts no in-app photo tool and never edits or retouches a seller's images.

It is also not a source of sample images. Every photo on a listing must be of the actual card the seller is shipping, taken by that seller. Screenshots from a pack-opening app, another marketplace's listing page, a supplier catalogue or a grading company's database are not allowed as listing photos, even when the card looks identical.

1The 8 shots

Shoot in this order. The first four are mandatory for any card; the last four depend on whether it is raw, graded or high value.

1. Front, straight on

The whole card, all four borders visible, filling the frame. This is the thumbnail.

Most common miss: tilting the phone. Keep it parallel to the card.

2. Back, straight on

Same framing as the front. Whitening, print lines and rebacked cards show up here.

Do not skip this because the back "looks fine".

3. Each corner, close up

Four close shots or one collage. Corner whitening is the first thing a grader sees.

Use the 2x lens and let autofocus settle before pressing.

4. Edge and surface, raking light

Hold the card at a low angle to a window. Scratches, print lines and dents appear that are invisible head-on.

No flash. Flash hides surface damage instead of showing it.

5. Slab label, readable

Graded cards only. The grade, the grader and the cert number must be legible at full size.

Do not photograph the label at an angle where digits blur.

6. Slab edge and holder

Cracks, scuffs and yellowing on the holder. Say in the description whether damage is on the card or only the case.

Tilt the slab so the case edge catches the light.

7. Packing, card in sleeve

Shows the card is already protected and that the seller knows how to ship. Builds more trust than a price cut.

Photograph a clean sleeve, not one with hair and dust on it.

8. Packed parcel, label hidden

The finished parcel, bubble wrap or box, ready to ship. Cover the address and phone with paper before shooting.

Never upload a label that shows a name, address, phone or tracking number.

3What never goes in a listing photo

  • Another app's screen. A pack-opening app, a live-break stream, another marketplace's product page, or a grading company's pop-report page is not a photo of your card.
  • Someone else's cert number. Cert numbers are public, which is exactly why they get reused by people who do not own the card. If the card is yours, photograph the label yourself.
  • Personal data. Receipts, invoices, delivery labels, order slips, bank screens, email screenshots, ID cards. Crop or cover all of it.
  • Other people. A shop assistant, a friend, a child in frame. Ask first, or reframe.
  • Claimed results. "Worth USD X", "guaranteed grade", "best pull" overlaid on the photo. State condition, not prophecy.
  • Stock supplier images. Even with permission, a catalogue render is not the card you are shipping.

4If you are photographing several cards

  1. Lay every card face up on the cloth under the same light and shoot the fronts in one pass.
  2. Flip the whole row and shoot the backs in a second pass, so front and back stay paired by position.
  3. Shoot corners and surface damage only for cards above your own value threshold, not for every common card.
  4. Name the files cardname-front.jpg and cardname-back.jpg before uploading, so a mixed batch cannot be attached to the wrong listing.
  5. Write the condition in your own words in the description: whitening, centering, print lines, dents, and whether a slab has case damage.

5Why this page exists

Trixovel is a marketplace for sellers, not a seller. It does not own the cards listed, does not photograph them and does not guarantee a grade or a value. The only thing the platform can do is make the standard for a listing photo clear before anyone uploads one, so a buyer knows what a complete listing looks like and a seller knows what is expected.

A listing that hides condition may sell once. A listing that shows it sells repeatedly and does not come back as a dispute.

6Questions

Do I need a professional camera? No. A phone in daylight beats a DSLR with flash for this.

Can I use the grading company's photo of my own card? No. Photograph the slab you are holding. Their image does not prove you have the card.

Can I use a photo my supplier sent me? Not as the listing photo. It is not the card in your hand, and the buyer cannot check condition from it.

What if my card is low value? Front and back is enough. The other shots are for cards where condition actually moves the price.
