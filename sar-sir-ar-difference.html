SAR vs SIR vs AR: what’s the difference?

These three labels mark the art-focused cards that collectors chase in modern Pokémon TCG sets. They look similar at a glance, so here is how each one works and how to tell them apart.

Updated 3 November 2026 · About a 5-minute read

Short answer: AR is the lower tier, with an illustration that extends across the card. SAR (Japanese sets) and SIR (English sets) are the higher tier, with a full-card scene that often includes a Trainer or a Pokémon ex. SAR and SIR are the same idea under two names.

How the names line up

The same two tiers are named differently depending on the language of the set. Japanese sets use the short codes AR and SAR. English sets from the Scarlet & Violet era spell the names out as Illustration Rare and Special Illustration Rare, which collectors shorten to SIR. When you see SAR on a Japanese card and SIR on an English one, they sit in the same slot.

How to tell them apart on the card

  • Look at the artwork first. If the picture stays mostly in the art box area, it is probably a regular card. If it spreads over most of the card but the card still looks like a normal Pokémon card, think AR. If the scene covers the entire card, think SAR or SIR.
  • Check the card type. A Trainer or Supporter with full-card art is a SAR or SIR. AR cards are Pokémon only.
  • Read the bottom corner. English cards print a rarity mark, and Japanese cards often print the rarity code. This confirms what the artwork suggests.
  • Check the number. Art rares are usually numbered above the printed set total, for example a card numbered higher than the number after the slash. See card numbers explained for why that is normal and why it is not proof on its own.

Which one is worth more?

In general, SAR and SIR cards are harder to pull than AR cards, so they tend to be more sought after. But value depends on the Pokémon or Trainer shown, the set, the language, the condition and what buyers want at the moment, and those change. Questions people ask

Are SAR and SIR the same thing?

They are the same tier with different names. SAR is the Japanese name (Special Art Rare) and SIR is the English name (Special Illustration Rare).

Is an AR a “full art” card?

Not in the traditional sense. The illustration extends beyond the art box, but SAR and SIR go further with a scene that covers the whole card.

Does a number higher than the set total mean the card is fake?

No. Secret rares such as AR, SAR and SIR are normally printed with a number above the set total. A number alone cannot prove a card is real or fake.
